I can talk about the different stages of people's lives.

Vocabulary Reference: Life Stages

▼
  • Infant /ˈɪnfənt/: a baby or very young child (trẻ sơ sinh).
  • Toddler /ˈtɒdlə/: a young child who has only recently learnt to walk (trẻ mới biết đi).
  • Young child: a child below school age or in early school years (trẻ nhỏ).
  • In his/her teens: aged between 13 and 19 (tuổi thiếu niên).
  • In his/her twenties: aged between 20 and 29 (tuổi đôi mươi).
  • Middle-aged /ˈmɪdl eɪdʒd/: between the ages of about 45 and 65 (trung niên).
  • Elderly /ˈeldəli/: a polite word for 'old' (người cao tuổi).
  • Centenarian /ˌsentɪˈneəriən/: a person who is 100 years old or older (người trăm tuổi).

Đáp án: She's a toddler
Giải thích: Toddler /ˈtɒdlə/ chỉ trẻ mới biết đi, thường trong khoảng 1–3 tuổi — đây là độ tuổi liền sau giai đoạn sơ sinh (infant).
2. He's
Đáp án: He's in his teens
Giải thích: Cụm in his/her teens dùng để chỉ độ tuổi từ 13 đến 19 (các số đếm có đuôi '-teen').
3. She's
Đáp án: She's middle-aged
Giải thích: Middle-aged /ˈmɪdl eɪdʒd/ chỉ người ở độ tuổi trung niên, khoảng 45–65 tuổi — không còn trẻ nhưng cũng chưa già.
4. He/She's
Đáp án: He/She's an infant
Giải thích: Infant /ˈɪnfənt/ là từ trang trọng/học thuật chỉ trẻ sơ sinh hoặc trẻ rất nhỏ chưa biết đi.
5. He's
Đáp án: He's a centenarian
Giải thích: Centenarian /ˌsentɪˈneəriən/ chỉ người đã sống đến 100 tuổi trở lên — gốc từ 'centi-' (trăm).
6. She's
Đáp án: She's elderly
Giải thích: Elderly /ˈeldəli/ là cách nói lịch sự, trang trọng hơn của từ 'old' khi nói về người cao tuổi.
7. He/She's
Đáp án: He/She's a young child
Giải thích: Young child chỉ trẻ ở độ tuổi mẫu giáo/tiểu học, lớn hơn toddler nhưng chưa đến tuổi teen.
8. He's
Đáp án: He's in his twenties
Giải thích: Cụm in his/her twenties dùng để chỉ độ tuổi từ 20 đến 29 (lấy theo hàng chục — 'twenties').

Track 1.02 (Exercise 3)

Interviewer In your opinion, when is the best age for young people to leave home?

Woman Well, it obviously depends on the circumstances. But I wouldn't advise young people to be in a hurry to leave home. The advantage of living with your parents, even when you're working, is that you can save money. It can benefit your parents too, as you can pay them rent and also help out with housework, and so on. But I would say that your late twenties would be a good time to leave home.

Interviewer What's the best age to learn to drive, do you think?

Girl Well, my brother learned to drive as soon as he could, at the age of seventeen. But he couldn't afford a car until he was 21. When he finally bought one, he'd forgotten a lot of what he'd been taught and had to have more lessons before he felt confident to drive. So, I'd say wait till you are in your early twenties, when you can afford a car.

Interviewer What's the best age to start a family, in your opinion?

Boy I think most couples wait a few years before they start a family, and I think that's quite sensible – it's important to settle down first and have a nice home. And most people find themselves in the situation when they're between about 30 and 35.

Interviewer In your opinion, what's the best age to buy a house or flat?

Girl Most young couples in the UK want to own their own home and they usually try to do this when they are about 30 or so, and they are settled in their careers. And I think that's about the right age. Before that, they don't usually have enough money.
