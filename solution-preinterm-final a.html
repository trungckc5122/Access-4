Reading

Living with Extreme Weather

You might think that people only worry about the weather during holidays, but in some countries, the weather is a serious daily problem. In Australia, for example, temperatures can reach over 40°C in summer, and in some parts, there is hardly any rain for months. Farmers suffer greatly, as their animals and crops often don't survive. These long dry periods are called droughts.

In other places, heavy rain can be more of a danger. In Southeast Asia, during the rainy season, strong storms and continuous rainfall cause floods, which damage homes, roads and schools. People sometimes have to leave their houses for weeks.

Meanwhile, in the United States, tornadoes are a big threat in some states. These strong, spinning winds can destroy everything in their path. Meteorologists use special tools to try to warn people before a tornado arrives, but sometimes there is very little time to prepare.

Many people now agree that climate change is making extreme weather worse. Summers are becoming hotter, and storms are stronger than before. That's why more schools are teaching students about climate and how to stay safe during dangerous weather. Even young children are learning what to do in emergencies.
